# SEO Strategy and Metadata

## Objective

Build search visibility with enterprise buyers who already recognise a transformation, programme, platform or industry problem. The strategy prioritises specific decision intent over broad, high-volume “digital transformation” traffic.

## Page-title pattern

- Home: `HACEY Global | Strategy, Technology and Transformation`
- Landing: `[Category] | HACEY Global`
- Detail: `[Specific capability, solution or industry] | HACEY Global`
- Insight: `[Article title] | HACEY Global`

Keep the most specific phrase first. Avoid repeating geography in every title.

## H1 and H2 rules

- One descriptive H1 per page.
- Use H2s for the buyer’s main questions: challenge, outcomes, services, approach, evidence, deployment, security and next step.
- Do not insert keywords into headings when they make the sentence less natural.
- Keep article sections useful when read as an outline.

## Internal linking

- Capability pages link to two to four relevant solutions and industries.
- Solution pages link to enabling capabilities and sector applications.
- Industry pages link to relevant solutions, technology ecosystems and insights.
- Articles link to one primary service page and one relevant sector page.
- Product concepts link to the enabling product engineering, AI, platform or programme capability.
- Breadcrumbs can be added if analytics show frequent entry to detail pages.

## Location strategy

Use factual, restrained geography:

- Organisation page: Austin, Texas, United States.
- Geographic focus: North America, United Kingdom and Europe.
- Create a location landing page only when there is a real service, team or operating presence to support it.
- Do not create thin city pages for Austin, Toronto, London, New York, Chicago, Washington DC or Amsterdam simply to rank.

## Structured data

Implemented:

- `Organization` JSON-LD with name, URL, founding year, email and Austin locality.
- XML sitemap generated from all routes.
- Robots rules allowing public pages and excluding the API route.

Recommended after launch:

- `Article` structured data with verified author and dates.
- `BreadcrumbList` on detail pages.
- `Service` only where the page describes a concrete service and not a speculative offering.
- `FAQPage` only for visible, useful FAQs that meet current search-engine eligibility rules.

## Open Graph and social

- Site-wide 1200×630 social image implemented.
- Site title and description implemented for Open Graph and X.
- Detail pages set title and description from the content record.
- Product and article detail pages clear inherited images when they lack a record-specific primary image.
- Create record-specific imagery only when it adds information and can be maintained.

## Technical implementation

- Canonical base: `https://haceyglobal.com`
- Responsive semantic HTML
- Metadata generated through the framework API
- `sitemap.xml` generated from structured content
- `robots.txt` generated from source
- Favicon support
- Descriptive page titles and meta descriptions
- Semantic headings and internal links
- Accessible navigation and forms

## Content quality controls

- No keyword stuffing or city-page multiplication.
- No unverified partner, client, certification or result terms.
- No product schema implying availability until products are commercial.
- Review title and description duplication quarterly.
- Update reviewed dates on evergreen insights after substantive review.
- Redirect retired routes rather than deleting their search value.

## Measurement

Configure after an analytics and consent decision:

- Qualified contact submissions by landing page
- Search impressions and clicks by capability cluster
- Non-branded search share
- Article-to-service navigation
- Detail-page engagement and contact intent
- Index coverage and crawl errors
- Core Web Vitals

Avoid treating raw traffic as the main success metric. The primary measure is qualified enterprise interest and evidence that target audiences understand the offer.
