# HACEY Global Brand Guide

Status: proposed identity system for review

## Brand foundation

### Positioning

HACEY Global is a strategy, innovation, technology and programme delivery organisation that helps organisations diagnose complex challenges, implement practical solutions and build the internal capability to sustain measurable change.

### Brand promise

Make complex transformation work in practice.

### Personality

- Intelligent, without performing expertise
- Calm, especially around complexity
- Direct, without becoming abrupt
- Collaborative, without losing accountability
- Practical, without sacrificing ambition
- Responsible, without relying on policy language alone

## Voice

Write as experienced practitioners speaking to experienced leaders.

### Voice principles

1. Lead with the decision, problem or outcome.
2. Use specific nouns and active verbs.
3. Explain what happens in an engagement.
4. Separate verified proof from proposed capability.
5. Prefer one strong claim to several broad ones.
6. Treat people, process, policy, data and technology as a connected system.

### Avoid

- Unsupported superlatives and global-scale claims
- “Unlock,” “reimagine,” “revolutionise,” “leverage” and “cutting-edge” as default verbs
- Repetitive “not X, but Y” constructions
- Generic urgency such as “in today’s rapidly evolving landscape”
- Technology as an end in itself
- Client, partner, certification, result or team-size claims without evidence

## Logo guidance

The current website contains an existing HACEY mark, but an editable master asset was not supplied. The project therefore does **not** recommend discarding or silently replacing it.

Before production:

1. Obtain the current logo in SVG, EPS or AI format.
2. Review legibility at small size, colour contrast and fit with the new positioning.
3. Retain it if it performs well; consider only a careful wordmark refinement if required.
4. Document minimum size, clear space, approved colourways and misuse examples using the final master.

The square “H” used in the working website and favicon is a proposed placeholder identifier, not a final replacement logo.

## Colour system

### Primary

- Ink: `#111C24` - primary text, dark surfaces, authority
- Warm Ivory: `#FBFAF6` - primary background, editorial warmth
- Signal Lime: `#B9F462` - calls to action, progress and focused emphasis
- Deep Teal: `#174F54` - sector depth, secondary surfaces, information emphasis

### Secondary and neutral

- Warm Paper: `#F4F1E9`
- Structural Line: `#D8D8CF`
- Body Slate: `#495963`
- Muted Slate: `#77858B`
- White: `#FFFFFF`

### Accessibility

- Use Ink on Warm Ivory or White for body text.
- Use White on Ink or Deep Teal for large and small text.
- Use Signal Lime with Ink text; do not set white text on Signal Lime.
- Do not communicate status through colour alone.
- Test any new combination to WCAG 2.2 AA contrast before release.

## Typography

### Primary sans serif

Geist, with Arial or system sans-serif fallback. Use for headings, body text, navigation and controls.

### Technical accent

IBM Plex Mono. Use sparingly for eyebrows, stage numbers, category labels, metadata and small technical annotations.

### Hierarchy

- Display/H1: 54-90 px desktop, 48-70 px mobile, 0.98 line height
- H2: 38-60 px desktop, 36-48 px mobile
- H3: 21-34 px depending on context
- Lead: 18-20 px, 1.55 line height
- Body: 16-18 px, 1.62-1.75 line height
- Label: 9-12 px mono, uppercase with tracking

Use sentence case. Avoid all-caps headings other than small labels.

## Graphic language

The brand visualises transformation as a system of connected decisions rather than a futuristic spectacle.

- Fine structural lines, nodes and pathways
- Controlled grids and numbered stages
- Large typographic statements
- Process and product-interface abstractions
- Asymmetric but orderly composition
- Signal Lime used as a decision point rather than decoration

Avoid generic neural brains, glowing robots, holographic hands, blue circuit boards and decorative gradients.

## Imagery

Prioritise, in order:

1. Real, approved images from HACEY engagements with accurate captions.
2. Product and service diagrams that explain the work.
3. High-quality documentary photography of relevant environments, with consent and usage rights.
4. Abstract branded visuals where a real image would add no information.

Photography should feel observational and composed, not staged. Show work, environments, tools and interaction rather than generic handshakes or posed meetings.

## Iconography

- Use Lucide-style outline icons at consistent stroke weight.
- Icons support comprehension; they do not replace strong labels.
- Use one icon per distinct function where helpful.
- Avoid large decorative icon collections and mixed visual styles.

## UI principles

1. First screens explain the page before asking the visitor to explore.
2. Whitespace creates hierarchy.
3. Borders and alignment organise content more often than cards or shadows.
4. Buttons use clear verbs and outcomes.
5. Interactive elements have visible hover and keyboard-focus states.
6. Mobile layouts are edited to one column with comfortable touch targets.

### Spacing

Base unit: 8 px.

- Control gaps: 8-16 px
- Component padding: 20-34 px
- Section spacing: 84-125 px
- Page gutters: 24 px desktop minimum, 16 px mobile
- Maximum content width: 1,180 px
- Reading width: 700-860 px

### Buttons

- Primary: Signal Lime background, Ink text
- Dark: Ink background, White text
- Light: White background, Ink text on dark surfaces
- Text link: sentence-case label with underline and directional icon

Minimum height: 43 px desktop and 44 px on touch-first interfaces.

### Cards

- Use flat surfaces, one-pixel structural borders and no default rounded “app card” style.
- Keep card headings distinct and descriptions concise.
- Use hover inversion only for important navigational collections.

### Forms

- Labels remain visible above fields.
- Required fields are evident through the form context and native validation.
- Error and success messages use text, not colour alone.
- Privacy language appears beside consent.
- Do not request information that is unnecessary for the first response.

## Data visualisation

- Ink, Deep Teal and Signal Lime are the core series colours.
- Warm Paper provides comparison or contextual ranges.
- Direct-label lines and bars where practical.
- Always include title, unit, timeframe and source.
- Use patterns or labels in addition to colour for accessibility.
- Never use a decorative chart where a number or sentence is clearer.

## Social application

- Use a 1200×630 canvas.
- Keep the headline to one clear idea.
- Use Ink background, Ivory typography and Signal Lime system accents.
- Include the HACEY Global wordmark with generous clear space.
- Do not add client logos, credentials or metrics without approval.

## Presentation application

- One decision or idea per slide.
- Use large editorial headlines and short supporting evidence.
- Apply stage numbers and system lines to methods and architectures.
- Keep body text above 18 pt and source notes readable.
- End with a specific next decision, not a generic thank-you slide.

## Document application

- Use a restrained cover with title, descriptor and date.
- Maintain a clear heading hierarchy and wide margins.
- Keep tables compact and avoid shaded decorative cells.
- Put legal status, version and review requirements on the document control page.

## Governance

- Brand owner approves new claims, proof, logo use and major visual extensions.
- Content owner verifies services, product status and sources.
- Legal or privacy owner reviews governance pages and regulated claims.
- Digital owner tests accessibility, performance and metadata before release.
- Review this guide annually or when the organisation changes position materially.
