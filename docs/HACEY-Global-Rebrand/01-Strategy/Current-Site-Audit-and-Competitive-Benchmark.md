# Current-Site Audit and Competitive Benchmark

Research date: 19 September 2026

## Executive finding

The current site presents HACEY as a social-impact consultancy with strengths in programmes, health, research, digital solutions, stakeholder engagement and systems strengthening. That foundation is useful. The repositioning opportunity is to translate it into a focused strategy-and-technology proposition without stretching evidence.

The new position should not attempt to mimic a very large global consultancy. HACEY Global can credibly occupy a more useful space: a focused implementation partner that joins strategy, technology, programme execution, organisational adoption and capability transfer.

## Current-site audit

Source reviewed: https://www.haceyglobal.com/ and indexed pages for `/services` and `/practice-areas`.

### Current positioning

- “Social impact consultancy” is the dominant category.
- The proposition joins high-level strategy and ground-level execution.
- Services include systems strengthening, digital solutions, fund management, project design and implementation, research, and human-resources management.
- Practice areas include health and wellbeing, economic inclusion, rights and advocacy, and environmental sustainability.

### Content worth retaining and reframing

- The emphasis on evidence and outcomes.
- Systems thinking and practical implementation.
- Programme design, research, health, stakeholder and community experience.
- The Understand → Design → Implement → Measure delivery logic as an early version of the HACEY Path.
- Claims about programme delivery, health work and expert networks only after supporting evidence is collected and approved.

### Content to rewrite or remove

- Replace the generic headline “Solutions for a Sustainable Future” with an immediately specific enterprise proposition.
- Remove placeholder “0+” metrics from live publication.
- Remove or verify anonymous testimonials before reuse; their current form creates a credibility risk.
- Do not repeat client or partner logos until permission and relationship context are documented.
- Remove the phone number from the new site unless ownership and continued use are confirmed. The supplied brief authorises email and city only.
- Replace broad phrases such as “cutting-edge” with concrete capability and operating outcomes.

### Visual and UX issues

- Repeated navigation and footer links reduce polish.
- The page hierarchy relies on similarly weighted service cards, which makes the central proposition harder to scan.
- Proof, services and practice areas compete before the buyer understands the updated offer.
- The current site lacks the enterprise-level information architecture needed for services, solutions, industries, technology, products and insights.
- Placeholder metrics and generic imagery weaken confidence.

### SEO and content gaps

- Page titles and headings are oriented to social impact rather than enterprise buyer search intent.
- There are no substantial pages for AI, cloud, CRM, programme recovery, interoperability or industry technology problems.
- Thought leadership is not yet functioning as an acquisition and credibility layer.
- Case-study evidence and illustrative approaches are not separated consistently.
- Internal linking cannot yet establish topical authority across service, solution and industry clusters.

## Competitive benchmark

### Valtech

Source: https://www.valtech.com/industries/

Useful practices:

- Industry navigation connects sector context with cases and insights.
- The page uses a short category proposition before showing detail.
- Related content creates a path from capability to proof and thought leadership.

HACEY application: use focused industry pages, but with fewer claims, less global-scale language and a stronger explanation of capability transfer.

### Educe Group

Source: https://educegroup.com/

Useful practices:

- Clear division between services, products, industries and insights.
- Technology is explained in business language.
- A memorable delivery line makes the service lifecycle easy to understand.

HACEY application: preserve the clear portfolio structure and product credibility, but avoid partner claims until verified.

### Konrad

Source: https://www.konrad.com/

Useful practices:

- A short capability architecture makes a complex firm understandable.
- Strong typography and generous whitespace create confidence.
- Work and outcomes are used as the main credibility mechanism.

HACEY application: adopt the simplicity and editorial scale, while using restrained proof boundaries rather than unverified results.

### Slalom

Sources: https://www.slalom.com/us/en/services/strategy/element and https://www.slalom.com/us/en/services/digital-product-building

Useful practices:

- Strategy is connected explicitly to implementation.
- Product work includes upskilling client teams, which validates capability transfer as a market-relevant differentiator.
- Industry language is specific to operational reality.

HACEY application: make capability transfer a visible part of service design, delivery and measurement.

### Thoughtworks

Source: https://www.thoughtworks.com/what-we-do

Useful practices:

- Capability groups are organised around the change the buyer needs.
- A named delivery method creates a bridge between brand and execution.
- Insights support the service architecture rather than sit apart as a generic blog.

HACEY application: use the HACEY Path as a concrete delivery system and organise insights around buyer decisions.

### Avanade and Publicis Sapient

Sources: https://www.avanade.com/ and https://knowledge.publicissapient.com/07/en-industries.html

Useful practices:

- Technology ecosystems are contextualised through outcomes and industries.
- Industry pages lead with sector challenges rather than technology lists.
- Services, solutions and industries use related but distinct taxonomies.

HACEY application: show capability across Microsoft, AWS, Salesforce, APIs and data ecosystems without implying formal partner status.

## Design and experience principles extracted

1. Explain the firm in one specific first-screen proposition.
2. Keep top-level navigation to six buyer-oriented choices plus contact.
3. Separate capabilities, solutions, industries and products.
4. Use the same proof discipline everywhere.
5. Let related insights deepen a service or industry path.
6. Use strong typography, restrained colour, high contrast and generous whitespace.
7. Replace generic stock images with diagrams, product-style visuals and evidence.
8. Design mobile pages as edited narratives, not collapsed desktop card grids.
9. Make contact language diagnostic and low pressure.
10. Use recurring language for implementation, adoption, measurement and capability transfer.

## Strategic opportunity

The rebrand can make HACEY Global legible to enterprise buyers without erasing its heritage. The most credible story is an evolution: work in complex health and social-impact environments created disciplines in research, systems thinking, implementation and stakeholder adoption; those disciplines now support a broader strategy-and-technology offer.
